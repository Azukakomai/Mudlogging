\chapter{INTRODUCTION}

\section{Background}

In the upstream oil and gas industry, subsurface exploration and well development carry immense capital risk \citep{ref3, ref9}. Drilling a single deepwater or exploratory well costs tens of millions of dollars; thus, asset teams urgently require continuous diagnostic insights to evaluate reservoir fluids as drilling advances \citep{ref3}. Globally, rotary drilling is a continuous, high-frequency industrial operation, with thousands of active drilling rigs operating simultaneously across global basins \citep{ref9, ref14, bakerhughes2024}. Because drilling occurs continuously on such a massive scale, Gas While Drilling (GWD) analysis is universally deployed as the primary real-time formation evaluation technique \citep{ref9, ref12}. GWD measures light-to-heavy hydrocarbon gas fractions ($C_1$ through $C_5$) liberated into the circulating drilling fluid as the rock matrix is crushed by the drill bit, providing exploration teams—specifically \textbf{well-site geologists}, \textbf{operations geologists}, and \textbf{petrophysicists}—with the earliest available diagnostic signature of subsurface hydrocarbon presence \citep{ref12, haworth1985}.

Despite the fact that GWD chromatographic data is acquired continuously on the very first drilling pass, standard field evaluation workflows remain fundamentally hindered by a major operational bottleneck \citep{ref9, ref11}. In conventional field operations, asset teams treat GWD data purely as a secondary monitoring record and routinely delay definitive hydrocarbon fluid typing (differentiating gas caps, volatile/heavy oil columns, and non-productive water sands) until post-drilling Wireline Logging (WL) or Logging While Drilling (LWD) suites are deployed \citep{ref8, ref12}. However, wireline logging requires pulling the drill string completely out of the borehole, resulting in days of non-productive rig standby time, severe borehole collapse and differential sticking risks, and hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars in service expenditures per well \citep{ref3, ref9}. If hydrocarbon fluid regimes could be reliably forecasted directly from first-drilling GWD data, wellsite and operations teams could make immediate geosteering corrections, optimize casing seat placement, or halt drilling early in non-productive structures before incurring expensive wireline logging operations \citep{ref3, ref8}.

A primary reason GWD data is not utilized for early fluid forecasting lies in the architectural and operational limitations of existing formation evaluation software \citep{ref8, wood2020}. Commercial platforms deployed across the industry—such as Schlumberger (SLB) Techlog, AspenTech Geolog, Advanced Logic Technology WellCAD, and RockWare LogPlot—as well as generic open-source Python scripts operate predominantly as \textit{passive data plotters} \citep{wood2020}. For instance, drafting software like WellCAD and LogPlot excels at rendering composite lithological symbols and strip tracks, but possesses zero built-in computational logic to derive ratio matrices or forecast fluid types. Meanwhile, comprehensive petrophysical suites like Techlog and Geolog treat GWD data as simple visual tracks; executing multi-ratio petrophysical forecasting requires users to write complex code in proprietary scripting consoles (e.g., Techlog Python Script Editor or Geolog Loglan macros) or develop compiled C++ DLL plugins \citep{wood2020}. Furthermore, these enterprise workstation suites are notoriously heavyweight, complex, and slow to set up—burdening geoscientists with steep learning curves, cluttered multi-level configuration menus, and multi-thousand-dollar annual licensing barriers that hinder fast-paced field operations \citep{ref8, wood2020}.

In addition to software complexity and latency, formation evaluation workflows are constrained by strict compliance with industry Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) and international regulatory frameworks. While modern data-driven machine learning (ML) architectures have been explored for well log prediction, their "black-box" nature obscures internal reasoning, rendering predictions non-auditable and indefensible during formal reserve certification audits under the \citet{ref3} Petroleum Resources Management System (PRMS) \citep{ref1, ref3}. Consequently, subsurface asset teams face a stark dilemma between non-transparent, audit-disqualified AI models and slow, monolithic workstation plotting software that cannot automate real-time GWD fluid forecasting.

This situation exposes a critical technological and operational gap: there is currently an absence of a dedicated, lightweight decision support platform that bridges raw first-drilling GWD data with automated multi-ratio petrophysical derivation and deterministic hydrocarbon fluid forecasting \citep{ref8, wood2020}. To resolve these challenges, this research develops a dedicated, open-source web-based decision support system using Python (Dash and Plotly). The proposed application simplifies the operational workflow into an intuitive, single-click upload-and-go dashboard tailored for well-site geologists, operations geologists, and petrophysicists, executing automated multi-ratio petrophysical calculations and deterministic fluid forecasting in seconds without proprietary software dependencies \citep{ref3, ref8, ref11, ref12}.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

While Gas While Drilling (GWD) chromatographic data is acquired immediately on the first drilling run, multidisciplinary exploration teams—encompassing well-site geologists, operations geologists, and petrophysicists—cannot utilize this data for real-time hydrocarbon fluid classification because existing formation evaluation software suites function merely as passive, slow-to-configure data plotters rather than automated forecasting engines. Consequently, operators are forced to either rely on ambiguous manual interpretations of oversimplified two-ratio charts or delay fluid verification until costly post-drilling wireline logging operations are conducted, introducing severe financial costs, rig downtime, and operational borehole hazards. Furthermore, non-transparent machine learning models fail strict industry SOP and SPE PRMS reserve certification audit requirements. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a simplified, open-source, web-based decision support system that automates the generation of multi-ratio petrophysical indicators and delivers deterministic, SOP-compliant hydrocarbon fluid forecasting directly from first-drilling GWD data in real time.

\section{Research Objectives}

The primary objective of this research is to design, develop, and evaluate a simplified, open-source, web-based deterministic decision support application capable of transforming raw first-drilling Gas While Drilling (GWD) chromatographic data into real-time multi-ratio petrophysical indicators and automated hydrocarbon fluid zone forecasts, eliminating the operational delays of post-drilling wireline logging, the audit risks of black-box models, and the sluggish complexity of passive log plotting software.

To systematically achieve this main objective, the specific research goals are formulated as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Multi-Ratio Forecasting Engine:} To develop a Python calculation engine that ingests raw chromatographic gas fractions ($C_1$ through $C_5$) and automatically computes 16 derived petrophysical indicators—including classic Pixler ratios, expanded Haworth show ratios ($W_h, B_h, C_h$), butane multipliers, and composite fluid indicators ($GOW, WBS, GOR$)—executing deterministic decision logic to forecast hydrocarbon fluid zones (Gas, Oil, or Water/Non-productive) at every depth increment.
    
    \item \textbf{Simplified Multi-Track Web Dashboard and Dynamic Controls:} To construct a lightweight, intuitive web visualization interface using Dash and Plotly tailored for well-site geologists, operations geologists, and petrophysicists, displaying processed parameters across 21 unique, synchronized depth tracks and equipped with dynamic pipeline controls (including interactive column configuration and formula editing managers) for real-time operational calibration.
    
    \item \textbf{Verification of Industry SOP and Regulatory Audit Compliance:} To establish documented proof of full compliance with petroleum industry Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) and international reserve certification standards by:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Hardcoding the calculation engine strictly with ratified petrophysical equations from peer-reviewed SPE and AAPG literature, including Haworth show ratios \citep{haworth1985}, Pixler boundaries \citep{ref13}, Whittaker composite curves \citep{whittaker1991}, and SPE GOR screening rules \citep{spe2020}.
        \item Fulfilling SPE/WPC/AAPG/SPEE/SEG PRMS Chapter 4 deterministic auditability mandates \citep{ref2, ref3} through 100\% mathematically traceable closed-form logic that eliminates the audit risks of opaque machine learning weights.
        \item Structuring multi-track log visualizations in accordance with API RP 31A and SPWLA wellsite mud logging reporting standards.
    \end{itemize}
    
    \item \textbf{High-Throughput Latency Optimization and Usability Benchmarking:} To optimize the end-to-end processing pipeline—spanning single-step raw file ingestion, 16-parameter ratio computation, and multi-track visual rendering—to execute in under 5.0 seconds across full well trajectories ($> 3000\text{ m}$), and validate system usability with practicing subsurface professionals to achieve a mean usability score of $\overline{S} > 68.0/100$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Benefits}

The development of a dedicated decision support system for real-time GWD hydrocarbon forecasting provides tangible operational, financial, and educational contributions:

\subsection{Industrial and Operational Impact}

\subsubsection{Early First-Drilling Fluid Intelligence for Wellsite and Operations Teams}
By forecasting hydrocarbon fluid contacts directly from GWD data during the first bit run, \textbf{well-site geologists}, \textbf{operations geologists}, and \textbf{petrophysicists} gain immediate formation insights days before wireline logging tools can be mobilized. This early intelligence enables proactive geosteering adjustments, optimized casing seat placement, and immediate well termination in non-productive formations, preventing millions of dollars in unnecessary drilling and rig standby expenses.

\subsubsection{Workflow Simplification and Speed Optimization}
By replacing monolithic, slow-to-configure workstation software with a streamlined, single-click web application, wellsite teams eliminate hours of manual script configuration and multi-step data preparation. The sub-5-second execution latency provides immediate decision support within tight operational drilling windows.

\subsubsection{Documented Proof of SOP and Regulatory Audit Compliance}
The software demonstrates complete compliance with oil and gas Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) through three tangible criteria:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Standardized Methodological Lineage:} The mathematical engine strictly executes equations published in standard industry literature and engineering manuals (\citet{haworth1985} for show ratios, \citet{ref13} for Pixler boundaries, \citet{whittaker1991} for continuous composite gas indices, and \citet{spe2020} for GOR classifications).
    \item \textit{Deterministic Auditability under SPE PRMS:} In full accordance with \citet{ref2} and \citet{ref3} PRMS Chapter 4 standards for deterministic resource evaluation, every intermediate calculation and boundary classification is 100\% mathematically traceable from raw input to final output, eliminating the audit failure risks associated with non-transparent machine learning models.
    \item \textit{Standardized Mud Log Rendering:} Output tracks adhere directly to standardized wellsite strip log conventions (API RP 31A / SPWLA standards) for immediate operational reporting.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Cost Reduction and Elimination of License Barriers}
By providing a free, open-source web application tailored specifically for automated GWD multi-ratio forecasting, this platform removes the requirement for independent operators, service companies, and consultants to purchase expensive enterprise software licenses (frequently exceeding \$20,000 to \$50,000 annually per seat) merely to perform routine mud log analytics \citep{wood2020}.

\subsection{Academic and Educational Contributions}

\subsubsection{Democratization of Advanced Petrophysical Computing}
Extreme commercial software costs frequently lock academic institutions and student researchers out of advanced log evaluation tools. Releasing this application under an open-source license democratizes access to multi-parameter petrophysical computing and reproducible research in geoscience departments.

\subsubsection{Enhancement of Petrophysical Pedagogy}
In petroleum geology education, students often struggle to grasp how shifting chromatographic gas fractions relate to complex ratio boundaries and fluid typing. This interactive platform enables students to explore multi-ratio models, customize mathematical expressions, and visualize live log curve responses across standardized wellsite templates in real time.
